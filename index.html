        How the calculations work

        Short guides to the methods behind each calculator, with worked examples you can check against the tools above.

        How to estimate bricks, cement and sand for a wall

        Standard bricks are 190 × 90 × 90 mm. With a 10 mm mortar joint each brick takes up 200 × 100 × 100 mm (its nominal size), so 1 m³ of wall needs 500 bricks.

        1. Find the wall volume: length × height × thickness.
        2. Bricks = wall volume × 500.
        3. Mortar (wet) = wall volume − the bricks' own volume (number of bricks × 0.001539 m³).
        4. Dry mortar = wet mortar × 1.33, because dry cement and sand shrink when water is added.
        5. Split the dry mortar by the mix ratio. For 1 : 6, cement is 1/7 and sand is 6/7. One 50 kg bag of cement is 0.0347 m³.
        6. Add wastage, usually 5%.
        Example: 20 ft × 10 ft wall, 200 mm thick, mortar 1 : 6, 5% wastage Volume = 6.096 × 3.048 × 0.2 = 3.716 m³
        Bricks = 3.716 × 500 = 1,858, plus 5% = 1,951 bricks
        Wet mortar = 3.716 − (1,858 × 0.001539) = 0.857 m³; dry = 1.139 m³
        Cement = 1.139 ÷ 7 = 0.163 m³ = 4.69 bags, plus 5% = 4.9 bags
        Sand = 0.163 × 6 = 0.976 m³, plus 5% = 1.03 m³ (36.2 cft)

        How to calculate cement, sand and aggregate for concrete

        The concrete grade sets the mix ratio of cement : sand : aggregate. The method is the same for slabs, columns, beams, footings and stairs; only the volume changes.

        1. Find the wet concrete volume, for example slab length × width × thickness.
        2. Dry volume = wet volume × 1.54, which allows for voids and shrinkage.
        3. Split the dry volume by the ratio. For M20 (1 : 1.5 : 3) the parts add up to 5.5, so cement is 1/5.5, sand 1.5/5.5 and aggregate 3/5.5.
        4. Cement bags = cement volume ÷ 0.0347. Add wastage.
        5. Steel is estimated as a percentage of the concrete volume × 7,850 kg/m³. Always use the bar schedule from the structural drawings for the real figure.
        Example: slab 20 ft × 15 ft × 5 in, M20, 5% wastage Volume = 6.096 × 4.572 × 0.127 = 3.54 m³ (125 cft); dry = 5.451 m³
        Cement = 5.451 ÷ 5.5 = 0.991 m³ = 28.56 bags, plus 5% = 30.0 bags
        Sand = 1.56 m³, aggregate = 3.12 m³ (both with 5%)
        Steel at 1% = 3.54 × 0.01 × 7,850 = 278 kg, plus 5% = 292 kg

        How to estimate plaster materials

        Inside walls are usually plastered 12 mm thick and outside walls 20 mm.

        1. Plaster volume (wet) = area × thickness.
        2. Dry volume = wet volume × 1.33.
        3. Split by the mix ratio. For 1 : 4, cement is 1/5 and sand is 4/5.
        4. Convert cement to bags (÷ 0.0347) and add wastage.
        Example: 400 sq ft, 12 mm, mix 1 : 4, 5% wastage Area = 37.16 m²; volume = 37.16 × 0.012 = 0.446 m³; dry = 0.593 m³
        Cement = 0.593 ÷ 5 = 0.119 m³ = 3.42 bags, plus 5% = 3.6 bags
        Sand = 0.119 × 4 = 0.474 m³, plus 5% = 0.50 m³ (17.6 cft)

        How loan EMI is calculated

        EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the fixed amount you pay each month until the loan is repaid. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones are mostly principal.

        EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1) P = loan amount, r = yearly interest rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100, n = number of months
        Example: Nu. 25,00,000 at 10% for 15 years r = 10 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.008333; n = 15 × 12 = 180
        (1 + r)180 = 4.4539
        EMI = 25,00,000 × 0.008333 × 4.4539 ÷ 3.4539 = Nu. 26,865 per month
        Total paid = Nu. 48,35,723, of which interest = Nu. 23,35,723

        About these estimates

        The calculators give quantities for planning and ordering. Real use depends on brick and aggregate quality, workmanship, site conditions and storage losses.

        1. Keep wastage at 5% for normal work; increase it for small or difficult jobs.
        2. Use designed mixes from a lab for M25 and above.
        3. Take steel quantities from the bar bending schedule, not from percentages.
        4. Have an engineer check the figures before ordering for a real project.
